What are Futures?

Futures are standardized contracts to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price on a specific future date. They allow investors to hedge against price fluctuations or speculate on market movements with leverage.

  • High leverage opportunities
  • Standardized contracts
  • Exchange-traded instruments
  • Daily mark-to-market settlement

What are Options?

Options give the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy (Call) or sell (Put) an underlying asset at a specified price before expiry. They offer flexibility with limited downside risk for buyers.

  • Limited risk for buyers
  • Multiple strategy combinations
  • Hedging and speculation
  • Time decay considerations

Frequently Asked Questions

Get answers to common questions about F&O trading

Futures are binding contracts where both parties must fulfill their obligations on the expiry date. Options give the buyer the right (not obligation) to execute the contract. Futures have unlimited profit/loss potential, while option buyers have limited risk (premium paid) with unlimited profit potential.

Margin is the minimum amount required to enter an F&O position. For futures, you need SPAN + Exposure margin (typically 10-30% of contract value). Options buyers pay the full premium upfront, while sellers need to maintain margins similar to futures. Margins vary based on volatility and contract specifications.

Index futures and options (Nifty, Bank Nifty) typically expire on the last Thursday of every month. Bank Nifty also has weekly expiries. Stock futures and options expire on the last Thursday of the month. If Thursday is a holiday, the expiry is moved to the previous trading day.

Lot size is the minimum quantity of the underlying asset that can be traded in one F&O contract. For example, Nifty has a lot size of 50, meaning one contract represents 50 units. Bank Nifty has a lot size of 15. Stock futures have varying lot sizes standardized by exchanges to maintain contract value between ₹5-15 lakhs.

While beginners can legally trade F&O, it's recommended to first gain experience in equity trading and thoroughly understand derivatives. F&O involves leverage, which amplifies both profits and losses. Start with paper trading, learn risk management, understand Greeks (for options), and trade with capital you can afford to lose. Consider our Financial Literacy resources before starting.

F&O trading carries significant risks: leverage can amplify losses, time decay affects option premiums, volatility can cause rapid price movements, and margin calls may force position closures. There's also counterparty risk (mitigated by exchange clearing), and the possibility of losing more than your initial investment in certain strategies. Always use stop-losses and proper position sizing.
